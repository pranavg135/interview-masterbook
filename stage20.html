01

Why this stage exists

Market sizing cases are the most structurally exposed case type in the whole Masterbook: there's nowhere to hide behind qualitative reasoning, because the entire answer is a number built from explicit, defensible assumptions. The discipline that separates a strong answer from a weak one isn't guessing a plausible-sounding figure — it's choosing the right sizing method for the situation (bottom-up population funnel, top-down category share, supply-side capacity, multi-sided marketplace, sizing-by-analogy for a category that barely exists yet, or triangulating multiple independent methods when the stakes are high enough to demand it), building the logic transparently, and being honest about which assumptions are load-bearing. This stage applies that discipline to 10 genuinely distinct sizing situations, from a straightforward consumer-durable bottom-up estimate to a full three-method triangulation for a fast-evolving global software category, so the underlying structural reasoning becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and try to pick your own sizing method (bottom-up population funnel, top-down from a known parent market, supply-side capacity, or multi-sided demand-and-supply) before expanding the rest. Market sizing cases especially reward being explicit about which assumptions are load-bearing and how you'd validate them with real data, since interviewers almost always push on the softest input in the chain. The 10 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 10 companies or sizing situations are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: building a defensible bottom-up or top-down estimate from explicit assumptions, cross-checking it against an independent method when the stakes justify it, and being transparent about which input is doing the most work is the same quantitative discipline behind the statistical and forecasting work in Stage 2 and Stage 10, and the structured-estimation habits used throughout the case bank. If an interviewer asks "have you sized a market before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a standalone market-sizing engagement, but I've built the quantitative habits — structuring an estimate from first principles, sanity-checking it against a second method, and being explicit about my assumptions rather than presenting a guess as fact — through real analytical work, and I've drilled a full bank of sizing cases across very different market structures to build the method-selection instinct (bottom-up, top-down, supply-side, sizing-by-analogy, triangulation) on top of that foundation."
