01

Why this stage exists

AI/GenAI cases are the newest and fastest-moving case type in the whole Masterbook, and the discipline that separates a strong answer from a buzzword-heavy one is the same discipline underlying every other stage: name the actual decision or failure mode precisely, reason about it with real trade-offs (latency vs. quality, autonomy vs. oversight, cost vs. capability, precision vs. recall), and back the recommendation with a structural argument rather than a vague appeal to "AI best practices." The pattern that separates a strong answer from a weak one is resisting the urge to treat every problem as a model-capability problem, and instead checking whether the real driver is a data problem, a retrieval/chunking problem, an evaluation-design gap, a governance gap, or a genuine model-quality limitation before proposing a fix. This stage applies that discipline to 10 genuinely distinct AI/GenAI situations, from a straightforward prompting-vs-fine-tuning decision to an expert-level post-incident guardrail redesign after a costly autonomous-agent failure, so the underlying rigorous, mechanism-first AI reasoning becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and before reaching for a solution, name which layer of the problem is actually at fault — the underlying model, the data feeding it, the retrieval/context pipeline, the evaluation approach, or the human-oversight design around it — since AI/GenAI interview questions punish generic "use better prompts" or "fine-tune it" answers that skip this diagnosis. AI/GenAI cases especially reward being explicit about the trade-off being made (autonomy vs. safety, cost vs. quality, speed vs. accuracy) rather than presenting a recommendation as if it were free of downside. The 10 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 10 companies or AI/GenAI situations are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: diagnosing which layer of an AI system is actually responsible for a failure before proposing a fix, reasoning explicitly about autonomy, cost, and safety trade-offs instead of treating them as afterthoughts, and grounding a recommendation in a structural argument rather than AI-hype language is the same rigorous, mechanism-first discipline behind the GenAI/LLM/RAG/Agents work in Stage 13 and the evidence-first reasoning drilled across every other case bank in this Masterbook. If an interviewer asks "have you made an AI or GenAI product decision before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a standalone AI-product role, but I've built the underlying habits — diagnosing whether an AI failure is a data, retrieval, evaluation, or model problem, reasoning explicitly about autonomy and safety trade-offs, and defending a recommendation under skeptical pushback — through real technical work, and I've drilled a full bank of AI/GenAI cases spanning deployment strategy, evaluation design, bias diagnosis, cost management, and governance to build that judgment on top of that foundation."
