01

Why this stage exists

Product cases are the broadest case type in the whole Masterbook, because "product sense" isn't one skill — it's prioritization judgment, metrics diagnosis, launch economics, retention root-causing, and build-vs-buy-vs-partner strategy all shown up disguised as a single conversational question. The discipline that separates a strong answer from a weak one is recognizing which of these sub-skills a given prompt is actually testing, structuring the specific lens that skill requires (an impact-vs-effort frame for prioritization, a funnel/segment breakdown for a metrics drop, a unit-economics model for a launch, a timing-of-churn clue for a retention problem), and backing the recommendation with real numbers rather than opinion. This stage applies that discipline to 10 genuinely distinct product situations, from a straightforward feature-prioritization call to an expert-level build-vs-partner-vs-acquire decision under real technology uncertainty, so the underlying product-judgment reasoning becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and first name which sub-skill the question is actually testing (prioritization, diagnosis, launch economics, retention, or strategic build-vs-buy) before reaching for a generic framework — the right structure follows directly from that classification. Product cases especially reward grounding a recommendation in a real number (a break-even calculation, a segmented impact estimate, a cost-benefit comparison) rather than a qualitative opinion, since "good product sense" in an interview is judged on rigor, not on having strong instincts alone. The 10 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 10 companies or product situations are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: classifying what a metrics or feature question is really asking, building a defensible model (impact-vs-effort, unit economics, segmented diagnosis) rather than reaching for intuition, and stress-testing a recommendation against a skeptical follow-up is the same structured, evidence-first discipline behind the analytics and SQL/Python diagnostic work in Stages 2-4, and the case-interview habits drilled across every other stage in this bank. If an interviewer asks "have you made a product decision before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a standalone product-management role, but I've built the underlying habits — structuring a prioritization or diagnosis rigorously, grounding a recommendation in real numbers rather than opinion, and defending it under pushback — through real analytical work, and I've drilled a full bank of product cases spanning prioritization, growth, retention, and strategy to build product judgment on top of that foundation."
