01

Why this stage exists

Operations cases are the most diagnostic case type in the whole Masterbook: unlike sizing or profitability cases where the deliverable is a number, an operations case's deliverable is a correctly identified root cause — and the discipline that separates a strong answer from a weak one is resisting the first plausible-sounding explanation in favor of actually tracing the process, checking capacity against demand at the right point in time, and using any natural experiment hiding in the data (a shift comparison, a component breakdown, a before/after) to distinguish between competing hypotheses rather than guessing. This stage applies that discipline to 10 genuinely distinct operational situations, from a single-outlet bottleneck diagnosis to a full enterprise-wide supply-chain risk-segmentation redesign, so the underlying diagnostic reasoning becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and try to generate your own diagnostic framework (a capacity-vs-demand check, a process-flow map, or a root-cause category set like the 4Ms — Man, Machine, Material, Method) before expanding the rest. Operations cases especially reward looking for a natural experiment hiding in the data — a shift comparison, a before/after, a component breakdown — that can distinguish between competing root-cause hypotheses rather than just asserting one. The 10 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 10 companies or operational situations are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: tracing a process end to end, checking capacity against demand at the specific point and time that matters, and using a natural experiment in the data to separate competing root causes rather than guessing is the same analytical discipline behind the SQL and Python diagnostic work in Stages 3-4, and the structured problem-solving habits from Stage 15. If an interviewer asks "have you diagnosed an operational problem before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a standalone operations-consulting engagement, but I've built the diagnostic habits — mapping a process, isolating where capacity or quality actually breaks down, and using real data to distinguish between competing explanations rather than asserting the first plausible one — through real analytical work, and I've drilled a full bank of operations cases across very different industries to build the root-cause instinct on top of that foundation."
