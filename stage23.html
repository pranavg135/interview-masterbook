01

Why this stage exists

Analytics cases are the most self-referential case type in the whole Masterbook, because the discipline being tested is exactly the discipline needed to solve the case: don't trust a metric or a dashboard number until you've checked whether it's measuring what you think it's measuring. The pattern that separates a strong answer from a weak one is resisting the urge to build a narrative around a number's face value, and instead checking for measurement artifacts, attribution bias, composition effects, and confounds before treating a metric movement as a real signal to act on. This stage applies that discipline to 10 genuinely distinct analytical situations, from a straightforward metric-definition check to an expert-level causal-robustness defense of an A/B test result under executive scrutiny, so the underlying skeptical, evidence-first analytical reasoning becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and before proposing any explanation, list the specific alternative, non-obvious explanations for the observed number (a measurement/tracking change, an attribution artifact, a composition effect, a confound) that a rigorous analyst would rule out first. Analytics cases especially reward decomposing a blended or aggregate number into segments and checking whether the pieces reconcile with the whole, since that reconciliation step is usually what turns a vague suspicion into a precise, defensible diagnosis. The 10 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

04

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 10 companies or analytics situations are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: treating a metric movement as a hypothesis to be tested rather than a fact to be explained, decomposing a blended number into segments to check for composition effects, and reconciling conflicting figures with real, quantified evidence rather than assertion is the same rigorous, evidence-first discipline behind the SQL, Python, and statistics work in Stages 2-4 and the A/B-testing methodology in Stage 11. If an interviewer asks "have you diagnosed a metrics or analytics problem before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a standalone analytics-consulting engagement, but I've built the underlying habits — checking a metric's definition before trusting its movement, decomposing an aggregate number to find its real driver, and reconciling conflicting figures with precise, quantified evidence — through real analytical work, and I've drilled a full bank of analytics cases spanning attribution, experimentation, segmentation, and forecasting to build that skeptical, evidence-first instinct on top of that foundation."
