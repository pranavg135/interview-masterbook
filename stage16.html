01

Why this stage exists

Profitability cases are the single most common case type in real interviews — almost every case-giving interviewer, whether at a consulting firm, a strategy team, or an analytics/product role, has at least one profit-decline prompt in their back pocket, because "profit is down, why, and what do we do" is genuinely the most common real business question. Stage 15 built the Profit = Revenue - Costs structure and the hypothesis-driven, MECE, clarifying-question, cross-question-handling discipline. This stage applies that toolkit to 20 completely distinct businesses so the underlying pattern becomes second nature rather than something re-derived from scratch every time — while each case's actual driver (labor cost, mix shift, payer mix, loss ratio, RevPAR, regulatory lag, and so on) stays genuinely different, so this is deliberate breadth practice, not the same case with the label changed.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and try to generate your own clarifying questions and MECE structure before expanding the rest — that's the actual skill being tested in a live interview, not passive reading. Once you've attempted your own structure, expand the case and compare. The 20 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 20 businesses are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases, exactly the way a real candidate drills unfamiliar case types before an interview. The honest bridge is the METHOD, not the specific company: the Profit = Revenue - Costs decomposition, the hypothesis-driven prioritization, and the MECE issue-tree discipline used in all 20 cases here are the same discipline genuinely used to structure 100+ raw Hero Electronics interviews into a prioritized thematic framework, and to break NanoCloud's ambiguous network-capacity problem into a structured, actionable set of workstreams (see Stage 15's Honest CV Framing). If an interviewer asks "have you solved a case like this before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not this exact business, but I've drilled a full bank of profitability cases across a dozen industries using the same MECE, hypothesis-driven structure I applied for real at Hero Electronics and NanoCloud — so the pattern is genuinely familiar even though the specific numbers here are new."
