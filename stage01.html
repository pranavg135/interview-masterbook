Risk / Gap Matrix

Step 3 of your master instructions: contradictions across CV versions, and places where the CV claims more depth than any bullet actually documents. None of these mean the underlying achievement is false — they mean the wording needs to be consistent, and the depth needs to be built before an interview, not discovered in one.

⚑ Wording inconsistency

Axtria — ownership verb on the $10M / ~60-rep bullet shifts by version

Consulting: "Led"Corp Strategy: "Directed"FinTech: "Built"Master / PM: "Owned"

Same underlying fact, four different ownership strengths. Not fabrication — but if you've sent a "Directed" version to one employer and then describe it in the interview using "Owned" or "Led" language, a sharp interviewer may probe the gap between the verb and the actual scope of your role on the team.

Fix: settle on one honest verb for your own head, independent of which CV version the employer saw, and be ready to define your literal scope (what you modeled vs. what the team decided) in one breath.
✓ Confirmed by Pranav — "Owned"

NanoCloud — "owned requirements & solution design" is the accurate claim

Consulting / Corp Strategy / FinTech / Master: "Delivered network optimization recommendations"Product Management: "Owned requirements definition and solution design" ✓ confirmed accurate

Confirmed: the Product Management CV's framing is the true one. That means the other four versions are actually underselling this role — "delivered recommendations" undersells the fact that you owned requirements gathering and solution design directly with the client, not just the output.

Fix: when discussing NanoCloud in any interview, regardless of which CV version got you there, lead with the "owned requirements & solution design" framing — it's both stronger and the one you've confirmed as true.
⚑ Selective inclusion

Certifications appear on one CV only

Product Management CV: IBM Certified Product Manager, KPMG Lean Six Sigma Green BeltAll other 4 CVs: not mentioned

If these are real and current, dropping them from four versions isn't wrong tactically (space, relevance), but it means you need to be ready to mention them naturally in a non-PM interview if asked "any certifications?" without it looking like you're improvising.

Fix: keep a short mental note of both, dates earned, and one sentence on what each involved — ready regardless of which CV version got you the interview.
⚑ Wording inconsistency

FDM Digital — "operational workflows" vs. "workflow product"

4 CVs: "Designed operational workflows for real-time item tracking"Product Management: "Designed and delivered a workflow product"

Minor, but "product" implies a shipped, owned artifact rather than a process design — worth having one consistent story for what was actually delivered (a tool? a documented process? both?).

Fix: low priority — just make sure your spoken description matches whichever framing, and can survive "was this a tool, or a process change?"
✓ Consistent — good sign

XGBoost / Logistic Regression is framed as "co-developed" on all 5 versions

Every version uses "co-developed a predictive / regression-based forecasting model"

This is the one modeling claim that's honestly worded everywhere — it doesn't overclaim solo ownership. That makes it a clean example of Level D "CV-defense" framing: you understand the methodology end-to-end and can speak to your specific contribution, without needing to pretend you built the model alone.

Fix: none needed on wording — just needs the real technical depth behind it, which Stages 7 & 9 build.

Allocation mechanism confirmed: linear programming + heuristics, both used

Confirmed: the ~60-rep allocation was driven by a combination of linear programming (formal optimization against constraints — territory, capacity, cost-per-call ceilings) and heuristic rules (practical adjustments/tie-breaking on top of the LP output, e.g. business-judgment overrides that a pure solver wouldn't capture). Prioritized Roadmap

Step 6: why the 33 stages run in this order — foundation before application, technical depth before the work-experience defense that leans on it, and interview simulation only once everything else exists to draw from.

Phase 1 — Foundation

Stages 1–6

CV intelligence, math/stats, SQL, Python, Spark, ML fundamentals. Everything downstream — the model chapters, the case work, the work-experience defense — assumes this is solid first.

Phase 2 — Core Quant Depth

Stages 7–12

Regression through XGBoost, forecasting/optimization, experimentation, BI tools — the technical spine your Axtria and NanoCloud claims sit on.

Phase 3 — Applied & Modern

Stages 13–26

GenAI/LLMs, Product Management, then the full Consulting track — structured problem solving/MECE/frameworks followed by a complete, full-quota case bank (Profitability, Market Entry, Growth, Pricing, Market Sizing, Operations, Product, Analytics, AI/GenAI, FinTech, Pharma) — the layers that map onto Hero Electronics, the PM-track CV, and case-style interviews.

Phase 4 — Personal Defense

Stages 27–31

Axtria, NanoCloud, Capgemini, Hero + Projects, then the Portfolio itself — built only once the technical foundation underneath each claim actually exists.

Phase 5 — Interview Readiness

Stages 32–33

The full question bank, mock interviews, then final integration — the 5/10/15-day study plans, gap audit, and cheat sheets, which only make sense once everything else is in place to summarize.
