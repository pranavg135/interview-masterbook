Claim 1 — The $10M / ~60-Rep Allocation

Priority Matrix rank #02, Level A, "Very High" interview odds, flagged High risk because the CV states the result without the method. This is the single most important story in the entire Masterbook to have cold.

"Owned data analysis for a revenue-optimization initiative, modeling rep efficiency and cost-per-call to allocate ~60 reps, realizing $10M in revenue."
Honest Scope

You owned the data analysis and modeling behind the initiative — building the rep-efficiency and cost-per-call features, running the allocation model, and defining the heuristic rules you'd recommend on top of it. The final heuristic overrides on specific accounts and the ultimate sign-off on the $10M validation sat with client and Axtria business stakeholders, not with you alone. That is a strong, honest claim — analytical ownership of a $10M-impact initiative — and it does not require pretending you had unilateral business authority you didn't have.

The Mechanism — Linear Programming + Heuristic Overlay

Confirmed hybrid method (Stage 1, Risk Matrix #6): a formal linear program produced the mathematically optimal allocation against real constraints, and a heuristic layer sat on top for judgment calls a solver can't make. This is genuinely how most real-world allocation systems work — not a textbook-pure optimization, and that's a strength to say out loud, not a weakness to hide.

maximize Σ_j u_j(x_j) # expected revenue uplift, summed over territories j subject to: Σ_j x_j = 60 # total reps available to allocate floor_j ≤ x_j ≤ cap_j ∀j # min coverage per priority territory, and a ceiling # where extra reps stop adding marginal revenue Σ_j (cpc_j × calls_j × x_j) ≤ Budget # cost-per-call × call volume stays inside budget x_j ∈ ℤ≥0 u_j(x_j) is a concave, diminishing-returns revenue-response curve per territory — estimated from the XGBoost uplift model (see Claim 3 below), not assumed.

The floor_j/cap_j pair is what stops the LP from either abandoning a strategically important-but-low-yield territory (floor) or over-saturating a high-yield one past the point of diminishing returns (cap) — both boundaries came from the business, not the solver. The heuristic layer sits after the LP solve: rounding fractional solutions, protecting a rep's existing account relationships from churn even where the raw math would move them, and folding in sales-leadership judgment on flagship accounts that a purely numeric objective wouldn't weight correctly.

Rep Efficiency & Cost-Per-Call — The Feature Engineering Behind u_j(x_j)

Two metrics, deliberately chosen as a pair rather than a single number: efficiency (revenue or response generated per call) captures the output side, cost-per-call (travel time, prep time, territory density) captures the input side. Optimizing on efficiency alone would reward reps in easy-to-reach, high-density territories without accounting for what it cost to get there; optimizing on cost alone would reward doing nothing. Together they approximate a per-rep, per-territory ROI, which is what actually needs to be maximized under a fixed headcount.

Answer Ladder — "Walk me through how the allocation was computed"

The specific three-depth format the master instructions call for. Practice moving between all three without notes.

30 seconds

"I owned the data analysis behind a rep-allocation project that reallocated about 60 reps across a client's territories and realized $10M in revenue. The core method was a linear program that maximized expected revenue subject to headcount, budget, and minimum-coverage constraints, with a heuristic layer on top for the judgment calls a pure solver can't make — like protecting a rep's existing account relationships."

2 minutes

Adds: what was broken before (reps allocated by static tenure-based rules, not by expected marginal return); my specific contribution (built the rep-efficiency and cost-per-call features that became the LP's revenue-uplift coefficients, ran the optimization, and proposed the heuristic rules); who made the final call on business exceptions (I flagged them with a recommendation, client and Axtria stakeholders signed off — that's the honest ownership line); and how $10M was measured (pre/post revenue compared across reallocated vs. non-reallocated territory cohorts, the same natural-experiment logic used throughout Stage 21 and Stage 23, isolating the reallocation effect from seasonal and macro noise as best the data allowed).

5 minutes

Full walkthrough in order: (1) the business problem — a static, tenure-based allocation was leaving revenue on the table because it ignored territory-level response differences; (2) the data — historical call logs, account potential, prior prescribing/purchasing behavior, territory demographics; (3) the modeling layer — an XGBoost model estimated the revenue-uplift curve u_j(x_j) per territory (see Claim 3), and a Logistic Regression model separately flagged which accounts were high-response-likelihood, feeding into how floor_j and cap_j were set; (4) the LP itself — decision variables x_j, the objective function, the three constraint families (headcount, coverage bounds, budget), solved to get a mathematically optimal starting allocation; (5) the heuristic overlay — rounding, relationship-protection rules, and flagship-account overrides, each one a deliberate, documented deviation from the raw LP output rather than an ad hoc change; (6) validation — the $10M figure checked against actual post-reallocation revenue by territory cohort; (7) what it became next — the same pipeline was productized into the self-service tool (Claim 2) so the client could re-run this without re-engaging Axtria every time assumptions changed.

Interviewer Cross-Questions — Technical
Q: How was ~60 derived — why not more or fewer reps?
A: It came from two directions meeting in the middle — the client's budget ceiling divided by fully-loaded cost per rep set an upper bound, and the revenue-uplift curve from the XGBoost model showed marginal returns flattening close to that same number, so ~60 was close to both the affordable ceiling and the point of diminishing analytical return. Draft — confirm exact tie-break logic with Pranav
Q: Why a linear program instead of letting the ML model drive allocation directly?
A: The LP guarantees feasibility against hard constraints — you cannot allocate a fractional rep, exceed budget, or leave a priority territory below its floor — in a way a standalone ML model has no mechanism to enforce. The ML model's job is to supply the revenue-uplift estimates that become the LP's objective coefficients; the two are complementary, not competing, and conflating them is a common wrong answer to this question.
Q: What would you change if you rebuilt this today?
A: Formalize more of the heuristic layer into explicit, documented constraints rather than case-by-case overrides — partly for repeatability, and partly because that's exactly what made the self-service tool possible: the more of the judgment layer that's rule-based rather than tribal knowledge, the more of it a client analyst can safely operate without Axtria in the loop.
Interviewer Cross-Questions — Business
Q: How was the $10M figure attributed and validated?
A: Pre/post revenue comparison across reallocated vs. non-reallocated territory cohorts, controlling as best the data allowed for seasonality and macro trend — the same cohort-comparison logic used to validate causal claims throughout the Analytics Case Bank (Stage 23). Worth being precise in an interview that this is the client-reported, business-validated figure from the CV, not a number recomputed independently — an honest interviewer answer distinguishes "I built the model that produced this" from "I personally re-audited the final $10M," and only the first claim needs to be made.
Q: Why efficiency and cost-per-call specifically, over other metrics you could have engineered?
A: They jointly cover the numerator and denominator of ROI per rep — output (efficiency) and input cost (cost-per-call) — which resists optimizing for a single vanity metric like raw call volume that looks good but doesn't actually track revenue impact.
Synthesis

This is the CV's single highest-value story, and it survives deep technical probing because the method is genuinely sound: an LP for the parts that must be guaranteed feasible, ML for the parts that need to be estimated, and an honest, named heuristic layer for the parts that need human judgment — with your own ownership scoped to the analysis and modeling, not to business authority you never claimed. See §08 below for how this resolves the CV's verb-drift risk directly.
