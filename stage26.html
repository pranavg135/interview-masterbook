01

Why this stage exists

Pharma cases close out the Masterbook's case-bank block, and the discipline they test is the most consequence-aware of any case type here, because a pharma decision almost never optimizes for a single metric in isolation — a pricing call has to survive payer negotiation and public scrutiny, a safety signal has to be weighed against the real risk of undertreating a serious condition, and a supply or trial-design problem has to be decomposed before a fix can be trusted. The pattern that separates a strong answer from a weak one is resisting a single-number, single-stakeholder read of a situation, and instead checking a proposed action against the full set of people it affects, patients, regulators, payers, and prescribers, before calling a recommendation complete. This stage applies that discipline to 10 genuinely distinct pharma situations, from a straightforward pricing-framework decision to an expert-level post-market safety crisis on a blockbuster drug, so the underlying rigorous, multi-stakeholder pharma reasoning becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and before proposing a fix, name every stakeholder a purely commercial or purely clinical answer would miss — patients, prescribers, payers, regulators — since pharma interview questions punish answers that optimize for one of these while ignoring how the others would react or be affected. Pharma cases especially reward decomposing a headline number (an adverse-event count, a real-world-effectiveness gap, a market-share shortfall) into its true drivers before proposing a response, since the headline number is very often measuring something other than what it first appears to. The 10 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 10 companies or pharma situations are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: decomposing a headline number into its true, often subgroup-specific drivers before proposing a response, checking a proposed action against every stakeholder it touches rather than optimizing for just one, and grounding a recommendation in worked, quantified evidence rather than a single confident claim is the same rigorous, evidence-first discipline behind the statistics and analytical work in Stages 2-4 and the analytical rigor drilled across every other case bank in this Masterbook. If an interviewer asks "have you worked on a pharma or healthcare-adjacent decision before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a standalone pharma-strategy role, but I've built the underlying habits — decomposing a headline metric to find its real, subgroup-specific driver, checking a decision against every stakeholder it affects, and defending a recommendation with quantified evidence under skeptical pushback — through real analytical work, and I've drilled a full bank of pharma cases spanning pricing, safety, clinical development, and market access to build that multi-stakeholder judgment on top of that foundation."
