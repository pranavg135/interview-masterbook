01

Why this stage exists

"Should we enter this market?" is the second most common case type after profitability, and it tests a genuinely different muscle: instead of diagnosing a problem in an existing business, you're evaluating a forward-looking, uncertain decision with no historical data to anchor on. That means market sizing, competitive structure assessment (Porter's Five Forces), and capability/fit analysis all come into play together, and the recommendation has to weigh attractiveness against the client's actual right to win — not just whether a market is big. This stage applies that discipline to 15 genuinely distinct entry decisions, from a simple geographic expansion to a technology giant considering healthcare diagnostics, so the underlying evaluation pattern becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and try to generate your own clarifying questions and MECE structure before expanding the rest. Market entry cases especially reward practicing the structure BEFORE seeing data, since real interviewers often deliberately give an ambiguous prompt to test whether you scope it correctly. The 15 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 15 companies or entry decisions are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: assessing market attractiveness (Five Forces), sizing an opportunity from first principles, and matching a strategic move to actual organizational capability is the same discipline behind the MakeMyTrip case competition's GenAI travel-bundling proposal (Stage 15's Level B evidence) — a genuine, competitive market-opportunity assessment under time pressure, even though it was a competition rather than live client work. If an interviewer asks "have you evaluated a market entry decision before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a live client engagement, but I've drilled a full bank of market-entry cases across a dozen industries and geographies, and the MakeMyTrip case competition required exactly this kind of opportunity-assessment thinking under real competitive pressure."
