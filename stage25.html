01

Why this stage exists

FinTech cases are the most balance-sheet-and-regulation-aware case type in the whole Masterbook, because the discipline being tested layers a normal business or analytical question on top of real constraints that don't exist elsewhere: fair-lending and disparate-impact law, credit-risk and interest-rate-risk mechanics, and the reality that a fintech's product decisions can directly create or absorb financial risk on its own balance sheet. The pattern that separates a strong answer from a weak one is never treating a fintech decision as a pure growth or product question in isolation — a pricing change, a model swap, or a market expansion has to be checked against compliance, credit-risk, and balance-sheet consequences before it's a complete recommendation. This stage applies that discipline to 10 genuinely distinct fintech situations, from a straightforward model-deployment trade-off to an expert-level funding-crisis response, so the underlying regulation-aware, risk-first fintech reasoning becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and before proposing a fix, ask what constraint a purely commercial answer would miss — a fair-lending or disparate-impact requirement, a credit-risk or interest-rate-risk exposure, or a regulatory-capital or licensing consideration — since fintech interview questions punish answers that optimize for growth or conversion alone without checking them against those constraints. FinTech cases especially reward grounding a recommendation in a real worked number (a loss-rate calculation, a margin bridge, a unit-economics comparison) rather than a qualitative judgment call. The 10 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 10 companies or fintech situations are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: decomposing a blended metric (a default rate, a chargeback rate, a margin figure) into its true drivers before proposing a fix, checking a commercially attractive decision against real regulatory and risk constraints, and grounding a recommendation in a worked financial calculation rather than intuition is the same rigorous, evidence-first discipline behind the statistics, SQL, and analytical work in Stages 2-4 and the analytics case work in Stage 23. If an interviewer asks "have you made a fintech or credit-risk decision before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a standalone fintech-risk role, but I've built the underlying habits — decomposing a blended metric to find its real driver, checking a decision against compliance and balance-sheet constraints, and defending a recommendation with a worked financial calculation under skeptical pushback — through real analytical work, and I've drilled a full bank of fintech cases spanning credit risk, fraud, payments, and balance-sheet management to build that judgment on top of that foundation."
