01

Why this stage exists

Growth cases are the natural companion to profitability cases: instead of "why did profit fall," the question is "how do we grow revenue/users/share by X% over Y timeframe," and the discipline that separates a strong answer from a weak one is decomposing "growth" into its actual MECE levers — more customers, higher spend per customer, higher frequency, new channels, new geographies, or acquisition — rather than jumping straight to a favorite tactic. Growth cases also uniquely test whether a candidate can distinguish SUSTAINABLE growth (that survives a competitor response and doesn't cannibalize existing revenue) from growth that just moves numbers around. This stage applies that discipline to 15 genuinely distinct growth situations, from a simple same-store-sales case to a technology platform's network-effect strategy, so the underlying decomposition becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and try to generate your own growth-lever MECE structure (the classic split is new customers × spend per customer × frequency, or acquisition × retention × expansion) before expanding the rest. Growth cases especially reward being explicit about which lever(s) you're prioritizing and why, since interviewers often push on whether a proposed tactic is genuinely incremental or just cannibalizes another lever. The 15 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 15 companies or growth situations are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: decomposing a growth goal into its true MECE levers, building a hypothesis about which lever has the most headroom, and sizing it with real numbers is the same discipline behind the product and metrics work at NanoCloud and Hero Electronics (Stage 14's Level A product-management evidence) and the MakeMyTrip case competition's growth-oriented GenAI travel-bundling proposal (Stage 15's Level B evidence). If an interviewer asks "have you driven growth before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a standalone growth-strategy engagement, but I've owned product metrics and prioritization decisions that were explicitly about growing usage and retention at Hero Electronics and NanoCloud, and I've drilled a full bank of growth cases across a dozen industries to build the broader lever-based thinking on top of that real experience."
