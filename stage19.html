01

Why this stage exists

Pricing cases are the sharpest test of quantitative business judgment in the whole Masterbook: unlike growth or profitability cases, where the lever is usually obvious once the MECE structure is drawn, pricing cases require genuinely modeling a trade-off — price against volume, discount depth against retention value, near-term margin against long-term position — and defending a specific number, not just a direction. The discipline that separates a strong answer from a weak one is building the actual demand/elasticity or segmentation logic and running real numbers through it, rather than asserting "raise the price" or "add a discount" without quantifying the trade-off. This stage applies that discipline to 10 genuinely distinct pricing situations, from a straightforward existing-customer price increase to a full per-seat-to-usage-based enterprise pricing-model redesign, so the underlying quantitative reasoning becomes second nature.

02

How to Use This Case Bank

Every case follows the exact same 10-part structure, so you can drill any single part in isolation or run the whole case end to end.

Prompt→ Clarifying Qs→ MECE Structure→ Hypothesis→ Data→ Calculation→ Insight→ Recommendation→ Risks→ Cross-Qs→ Synthesis

Recommended drill: read only the prompt, pause the page, and try to generate your own pricing MECE structure (the classic split is price × volume, or value delivered vs. willingness-to-pay vs. competitive anchor) before expanding the rest. Pricing cases especially reward being explicit about the actual trade-off curve — price against volume, discount against retention — and running a real number through it, since interviewers almost always push on the underlying elasticity or segmentation assumption. The 10 cases are tagged by difficulty (easier / medium / hard / expert) and filterable below the case list heading.

How This Connects to Your CV

None of these 10 companies or pricing situations are Pranav's own work experience, and none should ever be presented as such — they're deliberate practice cases. The honest bridge is the METHOD: modeling a price-volume or segmentation trade-off with real numbers, sizing a breakeven or elasticity threshold, and stress-testing the recommendation against interviewer pushback is the same quantitative discipline behind the statistical and forecasting work in Stage 2 and Stage 10, and the profitability-lever thinking in Stage 16's Profitability Case Bank. If an interviewer asks "have you made a pricing decision before," the honest and strong answer is: "Not as a standalone pricing-strategy engagement, but I've built the quantitative habits — modeling a trade-off, sizing a breakeven, and defending a specific number rather than just a direction — through real analytical work, and I've drilled a full bank of pricing cases across a dozen industries to build the pricing-specific structures (elasticity, segmentation, bundling economics) on top of that foundation."
